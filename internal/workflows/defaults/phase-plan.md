---
id: phase-plan
name: Plan, have the plan reviewed, then record when the code starts
states: [plan]
---
<id> is in plan. Before any code, write its plan, core first. The core is the one idea and the facts it depends on that the ticket does not already make obvious, in at most five sentences at the top of the plan. Prove each such fact now, against the real system: name the code, the recorded fixture, the log line or the measurement that shows it, and read or run it. A fact the ticket calls unmeasured, or one you read off a proxy, is measured before the plan rests on it. When a fact turns out not to exist (no record states it, nothing owns it), the plan changes its core: rest on a fact that does exist, or file the missing owner as its own ticket, make this one wait on it (`lit dep add <this-id> --blocked-by <owner-id>`), and do not design that owner inside this plan. Only then write the detail: what existing mechanism the plan touches, what it adds to it (state, timeouts, retries, locks, background work), and what it removes. Record it with `lit update <id> --plan-file <path>`, then give a reviewer with a fresh context (one that has not seen your work: a subagent, or a new session) the instruction "Law-review the plan of <id>: run `lit quickstart review` and follow it."

A fail sends the plan back to you. A finding is a symptom of a cause, so find the cause before you change anything, and fix that. If the cause is the core (a fact that does not hold, an owner that does not exist), go back to the core instead of patching. An answer that adds a guard, flag, stamp, special case or holder to cover a finding is wrong unless the core is proven and the addition is the only owner of what it guards; prefer the revision that removes or merges something. A reviewer's suggested cure is a hypothesis: check it against the cause before you adopt it. Rewrite the plan whole for the design you now hold, with no changelog, no "fixes v1's finding 2", and no text left from a design you dropped; `lit history <id> --version plan@<n>` keeps the earlier versions. When two fails in a row land on the same law or the same part of the mechanism, the core is wrong: go back to it. Have the new text reviewed.

`lit done <id>` finishes the ticket wherever its plan stands, and warns when the current plan has not passed. When you start writing its code, record it: `lit phase <id> --to impl`. A statusline and other sessions read the phase to see where this work stands, so it is only as current as the last `lit phase` you ran.
